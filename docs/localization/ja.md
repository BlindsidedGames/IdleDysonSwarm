# Japanese localization notes

The `ja` catalog targets neutral, contemporary Japanese suitable for a compact game interface. Control labels generally use short noun or verb forms, while feedback and explanatory text use polite, direct player-facing language.

## Typography and line breaking

- Use a Japanese UI font with full hiragana, katakana, kanji, Latin, mathematical-symbol, and full-width punctuation coverage. The current Latin font must fall back cleanly to a CJK font without changing numeric alignment.
- Japanese normally has no inter-word spaces. Verify that CSS line breaking allows wraps between Japanese characters while preventing awkward line starts with closing punctuation and small kana, and line ends with opening punctuation.
- Keep ICU arguments, rich-text tags, numbers, units, and formulas unbroken where practical. In particular, inspect compact goal rows, resource rates, skill-node cards, purchase buttons, and long Reality/Simulation upgrade descriptions at mobile widths.
- Japanese text is often shorter horizontally but taller when it wraps. Confirm button height, vertical centering, ruby-free font metrics, and fallback-font baseline alignment on Android, iOS, and the browser.

## Native-review flags

- Review the transliteration and capitalization of progression systems, especially インフィニティ, リアリティ, 量子跳躍, ストレンジマター, and the three cosmic mega-structures.
- Review the final readings of stylized skill families such as *Shoulders* and *Terra* in live UI; the latter remains in Latin as an authored naming series.
- Review Avocato/avocado and Avotation wordplay, the meditation hints, and deliberately absurd prose for tone rather than literal wording.
- Check counters for bots, buildings, planets, panels, and abstract currencies in their rendered contexts; compact UI sometimes omits counters intentionally.
- Review the long story chapters, Wiki explanations, save-recovery warnings, and large Offline Time disclosure for consistent politeness and terminology.
